2. App purpose and target audience

Chouwazi is an offline random selection utility for friends, families, and groups meeting in person. It helps people decide who starts or who goes next in everyday activities. Each participant places one finger on the same iPhone or iPad. After a countdown, one participating finger is selected at random, with an equal chance for each. This gives the group a quick, shared, visible way to make a selection.

3. Setup and access to the main features

All features are available immediately after installation. Review credentials and sample files are not required.

- Open the app on a physical iPhone or iPad.
- Hold at least two fingers in the selection area, away from the control buttons. The software supports up to ten participants, subject to the device's simultaneous touch capacity.
- Keep holding through the 0.8-second preparation period and 3-second countdown. One participating finger is selected, and the result remains visible.
- Before selection, adding or lifting a participating finger restarts preparation and the countdown. Moving a finger preserves the existing countdown.
- Lift every finger, then tap "Another round" to start again.
- With all fingers lifted, use the haptics control to switch vibration feedback, or tap "Privacy" to read the bundled offline privacy policy.

4. External services, tools, and platforms

Flutter and Dart provide the app framework and local random selection. iOS system capabilities provide touch input, display, and haptic feedback. The complete selection process runs on the device.

External online service dependencies: none.

The public support and privacy website provides explanatory information and contact details. The in-app privacy policy is bundled with the app and available offline.

5. Regional differences

The app provides the same selection features and user flow in all territories where it is offered. The interface follows the device language and supports English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese. English is used for other device languages. Selection rules and functionality remain consistent across these localizations.
